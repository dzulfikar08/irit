**A teaching toy, not a research result.** This page has nothing to do with irit's command-line tools.
It shows a textbook model of two species competing for the same resources, so you can see how growth
rates and competition strengths decide whether one species wins or both survive.

The model is the two-species competitive Lotka–Volterra system, solved in your browser with a
fourth-order Runge–Kutta integrator:

```text
dN₁/dt = r₁N₁ (1 − (N₁ + a₁₂N₂) / K₁)
dN₂/dt = r₂N₂ (1 − (N₂ + a₂₁N₁) / K₂)
```

N is a population size, r its growth rate when rare, K the size it settles at when alone (the carrying
capacity), and a₁₂ how much one individual of species 2 counts against species 1's capacity (a₂₁ the
reverse). The two preset experiments used yeasts and the ciliate *Paramecium*: those are the classic data sets
where every parameter was published. The rods and rounds in the dish are cartoons, not drawings of those organisms.

## Reading it

- **The dish** shows the populations at the time on the slider: more characters means a larger population,
  scaled so the larger carrying capacity fills the dish. Press Play to watch the run from the start.
- **Populations over time**: solid line for species 1, dashed for species 2, with each one's carrying
  capacity as a dotted line.
- **Phase plane**: each nullcline is where one species stops growing. Filled circles are stable end
  points, open circles unstable ones. Where the two lines sit decides which of the four endings you get.

## What the model leaves out

Real cultures have lag phases, toxins, spatial structure, mutation and chance. The parameters here are
constants; in Gause's own data they changed while the cultures grew. Treat the presets as a way to see
the model behave on real numbers, not as predictions about these organisms.
